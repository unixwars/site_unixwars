Wait, Unix What?

In the misty valleys of Bell-Labia, two sage archmages forged the First Scroll of the Kernel, written in the ancient runic tongue of C. For a golden age, scholars of the Berkeley Towers and merchants of the Guild of AT&T shared its magic freely across the Realm of Mainframe.

Then came the War of the Five Flavors.

More...Less...

Greed corrupted the High Merchant-Lords. Lord Sun forged a secret pact with the Grand Vizier of AT&T to lock the sacred runes behind golden vaults. Outraged, the Iron Barons of IBM, the Dwarf-Kings of DEC, and the Sorcerers of HP formed the Obsidian Syndicate in retaliation.

For seven winters, rival factions tore the realm apart. Wizard-engineers hurled incompatible libraries and cursed compilers across the divide. Illusions of Open Look clashed violently against the rigid wards of Motif, and spells spoken in the dialect of System V fizzled into ash inside the fortresses of BSD.

While the bickering lords bled their treasuries dry on proprietary walls, the Lich-King Gates of Redmond marched from the Frozen North with a monolithic army of Windows, threatening to conquer all.

Yet salvation rose from the quiet forests of Helsinki. There, a young hedge-wizard named Linus—aided by the bearded hermit Stallman—wove a brand-new tapestry of magic from scratch and gifted it freely to every peasant in the land. By the time the arrogant lords signed a desperate truce, their empires lay in ruins. The realm belonged to the free folk of the Penguin banner, who claimed the ancient magic without bowing to any king.

In the age of AI, shipping projects is cheap. Making one worth keeping is still priceless.

These are just some of the recent things I’ve been dabbling in.
